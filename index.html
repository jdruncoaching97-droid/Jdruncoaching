How coaching works

You start with where you are now, not where you were in your best season.

  1. Tell me about your running

    Recent races, weekly kilometres, injury history and the days you can train.

  2. Set your paces

    A race or time trial sets your VDOT, and every session is given a pace from it. Paces are reset as you improve.

  3. Train from your plan

    Each week appears in your athlete portal with the sessions, paces and notes. Log each run with distance, time, effort and any niggles.

  4. Check in weekly

    I read your logs and adjust the next week. If a session felt too easy or a calf is grumbling, the plan changes.

  5. Race

    A taper and race-week plan, then a review of what worked and what comes next.

The approach

Running is the main event, but it sits inside a plan that includes strength and recovery.

Paces from your VDOT
Easy, marathon, threshold, interval and repetition paces taken from Daniels' tables, using your current fitness.
Quality sessions that build in order
Repetitions and strides first, then threshold work, then intervals, then race-specific sessions.
Strength that supports the running
Heavy lifts paired with plyometrics, plus accessory work for the calves, hips and shins.
Load managed around your history
Long run and quality session sizes are capped as a share of your week, cutback weeks are built in, and past injuries shape how fast volume builds.
