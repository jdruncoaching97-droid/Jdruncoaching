Outer knee (ITB) pain in runners

Sharp pain on the outside of the knee, why rolling and stretching are not the fix, and how to rebuild.

By Joe, musculoskeletal physiotherapist and Level 1 Australian Athletics Run Coach  |  Updated October 2026

The short version

  • Outer knee pain is usually irritation near the outside of the knee, and stretching or rolling the band alone rarely fixes it.
  • Run below the point where pain starts, cut downhill and cambered surfaces, and keep pain at 2-3/10 or less, settling within 24 hours.
  • Hip and thigh strengthening is the main treatment.
  • Swelling, locking, or pain that does not settle in 4-6 weeks needs assessment.

What it is

Iliotibial band (ITB) pain is pain on the outer side of the knee. The band is a thick strip of tissue running down the outside of the thigh and attaching near the knee. Current thinking is that it is a compression and irritation problem near the outside of the knee, not simple "friction" or tightness, which is why stretching and rolling alone rarely solve it.

Who gets it

  • Runners who have increased distance, hills or especially downhill running too quickly.
  • Runners returning after a break.
  • Runners doing a lot of cambered roads or running on one side of a track in the same direction.

What it usually feels like

  • A sharp or burning pain on the outer knee that comes on at a fairly predictable point in a run, such as 20 minutes in.
  • Worse running downhill and going down stairs, and sometimes eases when you stop.
  • Tender on the outside of the knee, a finger's width above the joint line.

What to change this week

  • Run below the "onset" point. If it starts at 20 minutes, run 15, and build slowly from there.
  • Cut downhill running and cambered surfaces. Choose flat, even ground or a track, and change direction on the track.
  • Keep to the pain rule: pain of 2-3/10 or less while running, settling within 24 hours, with no night pain.
  • Keep fitness with cycling or pool running, if cycling is comfortable.
  • Step rate: start with a 5-10% increase on easy runs, then build gradually toward about 180 steps a minute (a coaching target, not a proven universal number).

Strengthen the hip and thigh

  • Side plank and side-lying hip abduction, 3 sets of 20-30 seconds or 3 sets of 12-15.
  • Single-leg bridge or hip thrust, 3 sets of 10-12.
  • Step-ups and split squats, 3 sets of 8-12, adding load slowly.
  • Calf raises, straight-knee and bent-knee, 3 sets of 8-12.
  • Foam rolling or stretching can feel good for a few minutes, but treat them as extras, not the fix.

Getting back to normal running

StepWhat to doMove on when
1Walking and cycling pain-free, strength work startedNo pain with stairs or daily life
2Short flat runs, below your onset point, every second dayPain 2-3/10 or less, settled within 24 hours, no night pain, for 2-3 sessions
3Add 5 minutes, then another run daySame rule
4Gentle hills, descents last, then faster workNormal training again

Common mistakes

  • Pushing through to "warm it up" and finishing the run in pain.
  • Relying on rolling the band as the whole plan.
  • Going straight back to hilly routes.
  • Stopping the strength work once the knee feels better.

Get it assessed if

Outer knee pain is usually ITB-related, but see someone if:

  • The knee is swollen, locks, catches or gives way.
  • There was a fall or twist with immediate pain.
  • Pain is constant, at night, or at rest.
  • It has not improved after 4-6 weeks of reduced load and strengthening.
